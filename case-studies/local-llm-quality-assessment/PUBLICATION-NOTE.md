# Publication Note

## Provenance

This public case study was reconstructed from two preserved historical artifacts:

1. an executive QA assessment / Go-No-Go report;
2. an evidence document containing screenshots and execution results from the original local LLM PoC.

## Public-portfolio treatment

The original files were not published verbatim.

The public version intentionally removes or omits:

- employer branding;
- local usernames;
- machine-specific paths;
- screenshots containing unnecessary local environment details;
- internal document-formatting artifacts;
- unsupported or overly broad claims.

The technical observations, timings, experimental scope, limitations and decision boundary were preserved.

## Why the raw evidence is not included

A professional portfolio should demonstrate engineering judgment without exposing information that does not contribute to that judgment.

For this reason, the public artifact favors:

- curated evidence over screenshot dumps;
- explicit limitations over inflated claims;
- reproducible reasoning over unnecessary environment disclosure;
- Quality Engineering decisions over vendor/model promotion.

## Attribution and scope

This case study represents Javier Capa's historical Quality Engineering work and learning from a local LLM Proof of Concept.

It is published as a personal portfolio case study and should not be interpreted as an official assessment, endorsement, benchmark, or current statement from any former employer, model vendor, or client.

## Portfolio purpose

The goal is to demonstrate the ability to:

- design an exploratory AI quality assessment;
- collect technical evidence;
- identify operational constraints;
- distinguish successful execution from production readiness;
- translate findings into an explicit Go / No-Go boundary.
